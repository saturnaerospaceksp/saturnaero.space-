Launch Rules

Launch Commit Policy

Rhea is GO when the stack is healthy, the orbital target is locked, Range has cleared the window, and the Iapetus / Rhea configuration is still matched to the mission.

The launch director keeps the vehicle in a clean state, with telemetry, staging, and command links green before terminal count continues.

Weather Criteria

Rhea can fly through more weather than the smaller vehicles, but the pad still needs enough margin to keep the climb and recovery safe.

  • Thunderstorms and CB clouds stay outside the launch chart.
  • Surface wind stays below the allowed limit for pad work and liftoff.
  • Fog is acceptable if the range picture remains usable.
  • Rain is acceptable only when the outside air temperature stays above freezing at cloud top.
  • Snow and sandstorms remain no-fly or no-test conditions.

Vehicle Limitations (Structural)

Rhea can take more load than the smaller stack, but the pad still needs a guardrail so the launch stays inside the approved envelope.

  • Rhea in any configuration can withstand +5g to -4g.
  • Rhea cannot deviate more than 5 degrees from its path.
  • If the stack is flexing hard enough to look wrong, stop and recheck.

Vehicle Limitations (Orbital)

Orbital limits are mission specific. If the target orbit changes, Range and the flight lead must confirm the updated envelope before terminal count resumes.

  • Do not commit to an orbit that violates the approved mission margins.
  • Keep the staging and recovery plan matched to the current flight profile.
  • If the target orbit is not locked, stop the count and re-clear the mission.
